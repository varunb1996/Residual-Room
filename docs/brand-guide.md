# Residual Room — Brand Guide (v1.1, FROZEN)

> v1.1 (2026-10-28): a "read next" pointer is not a call to action. See docs/decisions.md.

## What we are
An English publication of newsletters and explainers for engineers moving into AI and practitioners going deeper. We explain how AI systems actually work, and where they break, with sources for every claim.

**Tagline:** Learn the residual: the part you're missing.

## Voice
- **Engineer to engineer.** Plain, precise, no performance. Write the way you'd explain something to a sharp colleague at a whiteboard.
- **Calm skepticism.** We care about what works, what fails, and how we know. Uncertainty is stated, not hidden.
- **Failure modes are content.** Every explainer covers where the technique breaks.

## Tone by segment
- **Transitioning engineers:** build intuition first, using analogies from software engineering. Define every term on first use. End with "what to read next."
- **Practitioners:** skip the basics, lead with the mechanism or failure, go straight to tradeoffs and evidence.

## Structure
- **Explainer:** problem → intuition → mechanism → where it fails → sources → read next.
- **Newsletter issue:** one-line hook → the main piece → a segment-specific framing paragraph → one call to action.

## Terms and language
- Use standard technical terms consistently (e.g. "retrieval-augmented generation," shortened to RAG after first use).
- Don't anthropomorphize: "the model outputs," not "the model thinks" or "the model wants."
- Avoid: revolutionary, game-changer, magic, insane, secret, 10x, "AGI is near."

## Calls to action
One per message, chosen from: read the explainer, subscribe, read the source, reply with a question.
A "read next" pointer (e.g. "Read next: our First Principles explainer on RAG") is not a call to action and doesn't count toward the limit of one.
